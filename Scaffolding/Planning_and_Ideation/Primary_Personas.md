# Primary Personas

Three personas, in priority order. The app is built **for the coach first** — the
coach carries the pain. The athlete and parent are served in support of making
the coach's loop work end-to-end.

If we built the simplest possible version, these three personas — and nothing
more — are who it would serve.

---

## 1. Coach — *primary*

The person who carries the coordination burden today and whose mental load we are
trying to remove.

**Goals**
- Know, without effort, which upcoming days could support a session.
- Run a session with minimal back-and-forth: propose, collect commitments,
  confirm.
- Ensure safety-boat coverage before a session goes ahead.
- React cleanly when conditions change, without manually unwinding everything.

**Pains today**
- Repeatedly checking and re-checking conditions across multiple sources, with no
  single "is it safe to row?" answer.
- Posting WhatsApp polls and chasing responses to reach a viable headcount.
- Separately arranging a safety-boat driver.
- Having to manually cancel and re-communicate when weather turns at short notice.

**What they do in the app**
- See a calendar of upcoming days colour-coded by conditions (green / amber /
  red).
- Open a day to see the underlying metrics and the time rowing would be possible.
- Mark themselves unavailable on a day, or send out a session proposal.
- View a separate list of upcoming sessions and who has committed to each.
- Cancel a session before it happens — either pivoting to land training or
  cancelling outright — with athletes notified automatically.

---

## 2. Athlete — *secondary*

The member who actually rows. For underage members, it is currently the athlete
(not the parent) who responds to availability and then relays the details home.

**Goals**
- Easily see which sessions are available and commit to the ones they can make.
- Know who else is going.
- Be told promptly when a new session is proposed, or when something changes.

**Pains today**
- Coordination lives in WhatsApp polls; commitment and changes are informal.
- No clear single place showing what they have signed up for.

**What they do in the app**
- See a calendar of available days, including sessions under different conditions
  (e.g. weather is fine but no coach yet, vs. weather good and a coach has sent a
  proposal).
- Receive in-app and mobile push notifications when a session is proposed or
  changed (notifications must be enabled).
- Accept or decline proposed sessions.
- See a separate list of sessions they have committed to, including **who else
  has committed** — shown as profile circles, similar to a WhatsApp poll, not
  just a headcount.

---

## 3. Parent (of underage athletes) — *tertiary*

The parent who often has to physically get their child to a session, and so needs
visibility and lead time.

**Goals**
- Know when their child has committed to a session, so they can plan transport.
- Optionally, see the same session detail the athlete sees.

**Pains today**
- Reliant on the athlete to relay session timing, often late.
- Little advance certainty for planning lifts.

**What they do in the app**
- Subscribe to be notified when their child commits to a session.
- Receive a notification along the lines of "your child is planning to attend the
  session on [day/time]".
- Optionally view the athlete-level detail for a session if they want more than
  the notification.

---

### Priority summary

| Persona | Priority | Core need |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Coach | Primary | Remove the coordination/condition-checking burden |
| Athlete | Secondary | Easily commit and see who's going |
| Parent | Tertiary | Be notified their child is attending, for transport |
